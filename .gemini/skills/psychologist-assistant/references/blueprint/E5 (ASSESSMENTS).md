## Cognitive & Achievement: Area Definitions

### Cognitive Abilities

* Comprehension-Knowledge (Gc): This refers to understanding knowledge, particularly vocabulary, and reasoning using previously learned procedures. It also encompasses the breadth and depth of an individual's acquired understanding of a culture and the practical application of that knowledge. Gc is developed through formal education and general learning experiences.
* Fluid Reasoning (Gf): This is the ability to reason, form concepts, and solve problems when faced with a novel task that cannot be performed automatically. It involves manipulating rules, abstracting, generalizing, and identifying logical relationships. Gf is demonstrated in reorganization, transformation, and the extrapolation of information. Gf processes are minimally dependent on learning and acculturation.
* Short-Term Working Memory (Gsm): This is the ability to hold information in immediate awareness and then use the information within a few seconds. Gsm includes working memory capacity.
* Long-Term Storage & Retrieval (Glr): This is the ability to store information in long-term memory and to retrieve the information through association fluently.
* Visual Processing (Gv): The ability to generate, perceive, analyze, synthesize, manipulate, transform, and think with visual images, patterns, and stimuli.
* Auditory Processing (Ga): The ability to perceive, discriminate, analyze, and synthesize patterns among auditory stimuli. Ga also relates to phonological awareness.
* Cognitive Processing Speed (Gs): This is the speed and efficiency with which one can fluently perform simple mental tasks automatically, especially when under pressure to maintain focused attention and concentration; attentive speediness.

### Academic Skills

* Oral Expression: The ability to express one's ideas, thoughts, and feelings meaningfully.
* Listening Comprehension: The ability to receive, process, and understand auditory information.
* Basic Reading Skills: These are the fundamental reading skills, processes, and strategies required for utilizing the major cueing systems that lead to the comprehension of written material.
* Reading Fluency: The ability verbally and non-verbally read in a timely efficient manner.
* Reading Comprehension: This is the process by which the ideas and meanings intended by the author are constructed by the reader.
* Mathematics Calculation: These are the fundamental mathematics skills, processes, and strategies by which one shows an understanding of the means to reach a solution.
* Mathematics Problem-Solving: The ability to use mathematical facts, concepts, laws, and operations to solve mathematically based problems.
* Written Expression: The ability to use graphic symbols to communicate ideas, thoughts, and feelings meaningfully.

Comprehensive Test of Phonological Processing · Second Edition (CTOPP-2)
Phonological processing is the ability to see or hear a word, break it down to discrete sounds, and then associate each sound with letter(s) that make up the word. The examinee's phonological processing skills were assessed with the Comprehensive Test of Phonological Processing, Second Edition (CTOPP-2). The CTOPP-2 assesses three areas directly related to phonological processing: phonological awareness, phonological memory, and rapid naming. All three components of phonological processing are important for speech production as well as the development of spoken and written language skills. Additionally, deficits in phonological processes also contribute to difficulties in reading.

The Emotional Disturbance Decision Tree (EDDT) is a standardized, norm-referenced scale designed to assist in the identification of children who qualify for the federal Special Education category of Emotional Disturbance (ED). The EDDT is based on the criteria presented in the Individuals with Disabilities Education Improvement Act of 2004 (IDEA, 2004) and is specifically defined in the Assistance of States for the Education of Children with Disabilities (34 C.F.R. 300.7, 2002). The EDDT is completed by teachers or other personnel (e.g., school psychologists, clinical psychologists, diagnosticians, counselors, social workers) who have substantial contact with the student. The EDDT was normed, standardized, and validated for use with males and females ages 5 through 18 years. The normative sample included youth from a wide range of racial and ethnic backgrounds, geographic regions of the U.S., and residential communities (i.e., urban, suburban, rural). Thus, the EDDT is appropriate for children and adolescents (ages 5 through 18 years) throughout the U.S. from a broad range of racial/ethnic and sociodemographic contexts.
