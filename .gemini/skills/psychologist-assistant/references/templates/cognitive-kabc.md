The Kaufman Assessment Battery for Children, Second Edition Normative Update (KABC-II NU) is a clinical instrument designed to assess the cognitive abilities of children and adolescents for psychoeducational evaluations. The KABC-II NU is widely promoted as a fair assessment of children and adolescents from African American, Black, Hispanic, Native American, and Asian American backgrounds.

The KABC-II NU is a test of intellectual ability designed for use with children ages 3:0-18:11. The KABC-II NU was individually administered as a measure of general intellectual functioning or aptitude. The primary subtest scores contribute to the primary indexes, which indicate intellectual functioning in four (ages 3-6) or five (ages 7-18) cognitive areas that represent broad areas of cognitive abilities. Performance on the KABC-II NU is reported through several scores. These include standard scores, which have a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15. The classification of these scores ranges from the lower extreme to the upper extreme. Index scores between 85 and 115 are considered in the average range. Subtest scores between 7 and 12 are considered in the average range.

The KABC-II NU produces a global score, the Fluid-Crystallized Index (FCI) or the Nonverbal Index (NVI). The FCI is designed to measure general cognitive ability, emphasizing the complexity of children’s problem-solving and deemphasizing school skills such as reading, mathematics, and writing. The NVI can be used to facilitate the valid assessment of children who have difficulty understanding verbal stimuli, responding orally, or both, and for whom neither the FCI nor the MPI would be appropriate. Groups of children in which using the NVI may be most appropriate are CLD/EL students, children with hearing impairments, moderate to severe language disorders, autism spectrum disorders, and related disorders that make communication difficult.
